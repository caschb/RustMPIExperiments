#+title: LTO on both sides: Rust vs C/C++ plasma codes on Kabré
#+date: 2026-09-07

Purpose: pre-empt the talk question "what would the results look like with
LTO?". The deck says LTO was off on both sides and attributes the PIC gap to
un-inlined RSMPI wrappers. Both languages were rebuilt with and without LTO
by the same compilers (gcc 9.3.0, rustc 1.85.0, MPICH 3.3.2); every
repetition ran C, C+LTO, Rust, Rust+LTO back to back in one exclusive
allocation, so the comparison is internal to each job. Jobs 612036 (1 node),
612037 (2 nodes), 612038 (4 nodes), all completed, all 960 PIC runs
validated. Only the recorded 2025 flags were used (no -march=native, no
target-cpu=native).

* Headline

1. LTO does not close the PIC gap. C gains 2 to 5% from LTO (up to 10% on
   strong Linear at 4 nodes); Rust gains 0 to 3% and loses on strong Linear.
   With LTO on both sides the Rust-vs-C MAPE rises for Geometric, Sinusoidal
   and Linear, and falls only for Patch, where Rust already leads.
2. BS-SOLCTRA is insensitive to LTO in both languages (at most 0.4%), and
   Rust and C++ remain within 1% of each other.
3. The rebuilt no-LTO C binaries reproduce the 2025 rates within 2%. The
   rebuilt no-LTO Rust PIC binary does not: it is 2 to 5% slower in weak
   scaling and 10 to 25% slower at 4 nodes in strong scaling. See Caveats.

Standard deviations are below 0.6 s everywhere (n = 5). LTO has nothing to

inlined, and the runtime is compute. Figure: =figures/solctra_lto.pdf=.

* What this means for the deck

- "Where the PIC gap comes from" slide: the un-inlined RSMPI wrapper
  hypothesis predicts a Rust-only gain from LTO that scales with
  communication volume. Measured: Rust gains at most 3% and C gains more.
  The hypothesis is not supported; the slide needs a different suspect or
  an honest "still open".
- "Three answers" slide: "a gap that reaches 31% without LTO, and is most
  likely an upper bound" is contradicted; with LTO the weak Linear gap is
  37% over 1 to 4 nodes.
- Setup and Limitations slides: "LTO off on both sides" can now be replaced
  by "LTO on both sides changes the C rates by +2 to +5% and the Rust rates
  by 0 to +3%; the gap is not an LTO artefact". Also note the Setup slide's
  =-march=native= / =target-cpu=native= rows do not match the recorded
  builds (neither flag was used in 2025 or here).
- BS-SOLCTRA slide: unchanged; LTO is irrelevant there.

* Caveats

- Three node counts (1, 2, 4), not five: the kura-wide partition tops out
  at four nodes. The 2025 runs at 6 and 8 nodes are where Rust overtook C
  in strong scaling, so a strong-scaling MAPE over 1 to 4 nodes is larger
  than the deck's.
- The rebuilt no-LTO baseline reproduces the 2025 C/C++ numbers within 2%
  and the 2025 Rust weak-scaling numbers within 2 to 5%, but the Rust
  strong-scaling numbers at 4 nodes are 10 to 25% below 2025 (Linear 478
  vs 637 Mparticles/s). The 2025 Rust binary came from the spack rustdev
  environment; this one from rustup 1.85.0 with the same Cargo.lock. The
  2025 binary is preserved in =orig-binaries-2025/= on Kabré, so a
  back-to-back run of the two Rust binaries at 4 nodes would settle whether
  the difference is the build or the 2025 conditions. Numbers in
  =baseline-check-2025-vs-2026.csv=.
- Strong-scaling Rust rates at 2 and 4 nodes have large spreads (sd up to
  75 Mparticles/s at 4 nodes Linear), which is why the Rust LTO gain there
  has error bars crossing 1.0. Weak scaling is tight for both languages.
- The smoke test compared BS-SOLCTRA output files, but both codes only
  write the step-0 file (print period 10000), so LTO's effect on the
  trajectory was not checked. PIC validation (the PRK analytic check)
  passed for every run, including the -ffast-math C build.
- The C++ BS-SOLCTRA time is the stdout "Total execution time=[X]" value,
  which includes I/O and is about 0.15 s above the exec_compTime.txt value.
